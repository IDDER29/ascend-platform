In this project you are the control unit. Each lab below is the tiny CPU's datapath: its registers, its memory and its bus. Nothing happens until you switch on control signals and press Tick.

On each tick:
- exactly one signal from **onto the bus** puts a value on the bus (CO, RO, IO, AO or EO);
- every signal from **off the bus** that's on loads that value (MI, RI, II, AI, BI, OI or J);
- CE adds 1 to the program counter, FI loads the flags, and SU makes the ALU subtract.

Hover over or focus a signal to see what it does. Before each tick, say what will change.

## Part 1: Fetch one instruction

Every instruction starts with the same two steps. Fetch the first instruction: get it into the instruction register (IR) and move the program counter on to 1.

<DatapathLab caption='Fetch LDA 14' programJson='["LDA 14","HLT"]' dataJson='{"14":28}' goalJson='{"IR":30,"PC":1}' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Tick 1: **CO MI**. The program counter (0) goes onto the bus and into the memory address register.

Tick 2: **RO II CE**. Box 0 (0001 1110, LDA 14) goes onto the bus and into the IR, and the program counter moves to 1.

The IR now holds 30, which is 0001 1110: opcode 0001 (LDA) and operand 1110 (14).

</details>

## Part 2: Finish the load

Now run LDA 14 to the end: after the fetch, get the byte in box 14 into A.

<DatapathLab caption='Run LDA 14' programJson='["LDA 14","HLT"]' dataJson='{"14":28}' goalJson='{"A":28}' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

After the two fetch ticks: **IO MI** puts the operand, 14, into the memory address register; then **RO AI** puts box 14 (28) into A. Four ticks in all. The address and the value travel on the bus separately: first 14, then 28.

</details>

## Part 3: A whole program

Run all of LDA 14, ADD 15, OUT, HLT by hand, until the display shows 42. Box 15 holds 14.

<DatapathLab caption='28 + 14, by hand' programJson='["LDA 14","ADD 15","OUT","HLT"]' dataJson='{"14":28,"15":14}' goalJson='{"out":[42]}' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- LDA 14: CO MI, RO II CE, IO MI, RO AI.
- ADD 15: CO MI, RO II CE, IO MI, RO BI, EO AI FI.
- OUT: CO MI, RO II CE, AO OI.

That's 12 ticks to the display showing 42 (and 3 more for HLT). Notice EO AI: the ALU always shows A + B, and EO is what lets A load it. FI saves the flags at the same moment.

</details>

## Part 4: Store a value

Run LDI 9, STA 13 so that box 13 holds 9.

<DatapathLab caption='LDI 9, then STA 13' programJson='["LDI 9","STA 13","HLT"]' goalJson='{"mem":{"13":9}}' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- LDI 9: CO MI, RO II CE, then **IO AI**: the operand itself (9) goes into A, not the box it would point at.
- STA 13: CO MI, RO II CE, **IO MI**, **AO RI**: the address goes into the MAR first, then A is written into the box it points at.

Seven ticks. RI writes to whichever box the MAR points at, so the MAR has to be set on an earlier tick.

</details>

## Part 5: Break the bus rule

In any lab above, switch on two signals from the "onto the bus" row, say AO and CO, and press Tick. What happens, and why?

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

The lab refuses, and nothing changes: "AO and CO both try to put a value on the bus". The bus is one set of eight wires, and two values on it at once would be neither value. Making sure that never happens is a large part of the control unit's job.

</details>

<KeyIdea>An instruction is a list of ticks, and each tick is one value moving across the bus. Choosing the signals for each tick is what a control unit does.</KeyIdea>
