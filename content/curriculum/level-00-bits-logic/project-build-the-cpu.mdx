In "Build the datapath" you were the control unit, choosing signals tick by tick. Now you'll write those choices down as a **control table**: for the fetch and for each instruction, the signals for each step. The lab runs five real programs on your table and compares every result (the display, A and all of memory) with the reference tiny CPU.

Each line is one instruction: `NAME: signals | signals | ...`, one group of signals per tick.

The table starts with the fetch, LDA and HLT. Every program fails until you add more. Click a program to see its trace: every tick, with its signals and the value on the bus.

## Part 1: Add and show

Add lines for ADD and OUT, so that "28 + 14" shows 42.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- `ADD: IO MI | RO BI | EO AI FI`
- `OUT: AO OI`

ADD needs the operand's address in the MAR, then that box in B, then the ALU's sum back into A, with FI saving the flags on the same tick.

</details>

## Part 2: Subtract, load a number, store

Add SUB, LDI and STA. "Store and load" should now pass.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- `SUB: IO MI | RO BI | EO AI SU FI`: the same as ADD, plus SU.
- `LDI: IO AI`: the operand itself goes into A.
- `STA: IO MI | AO RI`: the address into the MAR, then A into that box.

</details>

## Part 3: Decisions

Add JMP, JZ and JC, and NOP for completeness. A step that should only happen when a flag is set ends with `if Z` or `if C`. All five programs should pass.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- `JMP: IO J`: the operand goes into the program counter.
- `JZ: IO J if Z`
- `JC: IO J if C`
- `NOP:` (no steps at all)

When the flag isn't set, the conditional step is a tick with no signals, and the program counter, already moved on during the fetch, points at the next instruction. That's the whole of how a CPU makes decisions.

</details>

<KeyIdea>A control unit is a table: for each instruction, which signals to switch on at each step. Write the table, and the datapath does the rest.</KeyIdea>

## Part 4: Count the ticks

With all five programs passing, look at the tick counts. "28 + 14" takes 15 ticks for 4 instructions. Which instructions take the most ticks, and why?

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

ADD and SUB take 5 ticks (2 for the fetch, 3 to execute), LDA and STA take 4, and OUT, LDI and JMP take 3. HLT stops the clock on its third tick. The instructions that touch memory and the ALU need more trips across the single bus. That count, ticks per instruction, is one of the first things computer architects try to reduce.

</details>

## Part 5: Find the bug from the trace

Change the fetch line to `FETCH: CO MI | RO II` (delete CE). Open the countdown program's trace. What do you see, and how does it point at the bug?

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Every program now fails. The countdown's trace shows the program counter's value, 0, going onto the bus at every fetch: the CPU fetches LDI 3 over and over and never reaches anything else. Nothing moves the program counter on, which points straight at the missing CE. Put it back.

</details>

## Done when

All five programs pass with your own table, and you can explain why JZ only needs one conditional step. You've built every part of a CPU: the ALU from gates, registers from flip-flops, memory with a decoder, and now its control unit.
