In this course you've met the four jobs every computer does, the difference between hardware and software, and the idea that a machine moves through states one step at a time. This project puts all of that on one map, a <b>Machine Atlas</b>, for real devices.

For each part, write your answer first, then open "Check your answer".

## Part 1: Map a phone

Here is a phone, sorted into five boxes:

<TruthTable caption='A phone, as a Machine Atlas' rowsJson='[["box", "the job", "the parts in a phone"], ["input", "takes information in", "touchscreen, microphone, camera, radio receiving"], ["processing", "works on information", "the CPU, following a program"], ["memory", "holds what is being worked on now", "RAM, which forgets when the power goes"], ["storage", "keeps information for later", "flash storage: photos, apps, messages"], ["output", "sends information out", "screen, speaker, radio sending"]]' />

Notice that the radio appears twice. Receiving is input and sending is output. A part belongs to a box because of the job it's doing at that moment, not because of what it looks like.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

If you put the radio in only one box, look again at what it does: it brings messages in and sends them out, so it's both. The same goes for a touchscreen with haptic feedback: touch is input, and the buzz is output.

</details>

## Part 2: Trace one action

Now follow one action through the map: you type "hi" and press send. Each step changes something and leaves everything else as it was.

<TruthTable caption='Sending a message, step by step' rowsJson='[["step", "box", "what changes", "what stays the same"], ["1", "input", "the screen senses taps on h, i and send", "nothing is stored yet"], ["2", "processing", "the CPU turns the taps into the letters h and i", "storage is untouched"], ["3", "memory", "RAM now holds the message being sent", "the old messages in storage"], ["4", "output", "the radio sends the message", "RAM still holds a copy"], ["5", "storage", "the message is saved to the chat history", "the screen still shows the chat"], ["6", "output", "the screen shows the message as sent", "storage keeps it for later"]]' />

Before you open the answer, say why step 5 matters if the phone is switched off right after step 6.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Without step 5 the message would exist only in RAM, and RAM forgets when the power goes off. Because it was written to storage, it's still in your chat history when the phone turns back on. Memory is for now; storage is for later.

</details>

## Part 3: Map a microwave

A microwave doesn't look like a computer, but it has a small chip inside running a program. Make its Machine Atlas: the five boxes, and the parts in each. Then trace this action: you press 3, 0, then start, and it heats for 30 seconds.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- Input: the keypad, and the door switch.
- Processing: a small chip, a microcontroller, running the microwave's program.
- Memory: the chip's own small memory, holding the time left.
- Storage: the program itself, kept in the chip so it survives a power cut.
- Output: the display, the beeper, the light, and the heater (the magnetron).

The trace: the keypad senses 3, 0 and start; the chip turns them into "30 seconds" and puts it in memory; the heater turns on; every second the chip takes 1 off the time in memory and the display shows it; at 0 the heater turns off and the beeper sounds.

It follows instructions, keeps state (the time left) and changes it step by step. That makes it a computer, just one built for a single job.

</details>

## Part 4: Fix the trace

Here is someone's trace for the microwave. One step is in the wrong place:

<TruthTable caption='A broken trace' rowsJson='[["step", "box", "what changes"], ["1", "input", "the keypad senses 3 and 0"], ["2", "output", "the display shows 0:30"], ["3", "processing", "the chip turns 3, 0 into 30 seconds"], ["4", "memory", "the time left is set to 30 seconds"]]' />

Which step is out of order, and why can't it happen there?

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Step 2. The display can't show 0:30 before the chip has worked out that the keys mean 30 seconds. Output only shows a result that processing has already produced. The right order is input, processing, memory, then output: keypad, chip, time left, display.

</details>
