This project is practice with a checker. Do every part on paper first, then check your answers in the Byte Lab at the end of the page. Only open "Check your answer" after you've compared.

## Part 1: Convert by hand

Fill in the gaps. Each row is one number, written three ways.

<TruthTable caption='Fill in the gaps' rowsJson='[["decimal", "binary", "hex"], ["45", "?", "?"], ["?", "1100 1000", "?"], ["?", "?", "7F"], ["?", "1001 0110", "?"]]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- 45 is 0010 1101, which is 2D.
- 1100 1000 is 200, which is C8.
- 7F is 0111 1111, which is 127.
- 1001 0110 is 150, which is 96. A hex 96 is not the decimal number 96: each hex digit is four bits, and 9 and 6 are 1001 and 0110.

</details>

## Part 2: One byte, two numbers

Read each byte twice: as an unsigned number, and as an 8-bit two's complement number.

<TruthTable caption='Two readings of each byte' rowsJson='[["byte", "unsigned", "signed"], ["1111 1111", "?", "?"], ["1000 0000", "?", "?"], ["0111 1111", "?", "?"], ["1100 1000", "?", "?"]]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- 1111 1111 is 255 unsigned and -1 signed.
- 1000 0000 is 128 unsigned and -128 signed, the most negative byte.
- 0111 1111 is 127 either way: the top bit is 0, so the two readings agree.
- 1100 1000 is 200 unsigned and -56 signed (-128 + 64 + 8).

The two readings differ only when the top bit is 1. Nothing in the byte says which reading is meant.

</details>

## Part 3: Carry or overflow?

For each 8-bit addition, predict three things: the byte you end up with, whether a carry comes out of the top bit (the unsigned verdict), and whether the signed answer is wrong (the signed verdict).

<TruthTable caption='Four additions, two verdicts each' rowsJson='[["sum", "result byte", "carry out?", "signed overflow?"], ["100 + 100", "?", "?", "?"], ["200 + 100", "?", "?", "?"], ["60 + 70", "?", "?", "?"], ["250 + 10", "?", "?", "?"]]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- 100 + 100 gives 200 (1100 1000). No carry, since 200 fits. But signed, 100 + 100 reads as -56: a signed overflow.
- 200 + 100 gives 44, with a carry out (300 doesn't fit). Signed, that's -56 + 100 = 44, which is right: no signed overflow.
- 60 + 70 gives 130. No carry, but signed it reads as -126: a signed overflow.
- 250 + 10 gives 4, with a carry. Signed, -6 + 10 = 4: right.

A carry and a signed overflow are separate questions about the same bits. Each can happen without the other.

</details>

<KeyIdea>The adder gives one byte. Whether that byte is the right answer depends on whether you meant unsigned or signed numbers.</KeyIdea>
