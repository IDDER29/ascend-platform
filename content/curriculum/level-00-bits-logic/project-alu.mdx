An ALU computes several things at once and passes on the one an operation code asks for. You'll build one bit of it, a <b>slice</b>, and the Gate Lab will chain eight slices into an 8-bit ALU and run it on real bytes.

The slice has five inputs: A and B (one bit of each number), CIN (the carry from the slice to the right), and a two-bit operation code, OP1 and OP0:

<TruthTable caption='The operation code' rowsJson='[["OP1", "OP0", "operation"], ["0", "0", "ADD: A + B"], ["0", "1", "SUB: A - B"], ["1", "0", "AND"], ["1", "1", "OR"]]' />

It has two outputs: OUT (this bit of the result) and COUT (the carry to the next slice). Work through the parts in the one Gate Lab below; it checks all 32 rows, and its bottom panel runs your slice chained eight times.

## Part 1: The adder's carry

The starting lines already compute SUM, the full adder's sum bit, from BB (which is just B while OP0 is 0). COUT is wrong: it's always 0. Make COUT the full adder's carry out, using A, BB and CIN.

Then set the chain panel to OP1 = 0, OP0 = 0 and try 28 + 14, and 200 + 100.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

`COUT = (A AND BB) OR (CIN AND (A XOR BB))`: a carry comes out when both bits are 1, or when exactly one is 1 and a carry came in. 28 + 14 gives 42. 200 + 100 gives 44 with COUT = 1, because 300 doesn't fit in 8 bits.

</details>

## Part 2: Subtract with the same adder

Set OP0 = 1 (keeping OP1 = 0) and try 28 - 14. You shouldn't need to change anything. Why does it work?

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

It gives 14. With OP0 = 1, `BB = B XOR OP0` inverts every bit of B, and the chain panel feeds OP0 into the first slice as its carry in. So the adder computes A + NOT B + 1, which is A - B in two's complement. Try 3 - 5: you get 254, the byte for -2.

</details>

## Part 3: The logic operations

LOGIC should be A AND B when OP0 is 0, and A OR B when OP0 is 1. That's a multiplexer choosing between two results by OP0. Rewrite the LOGIC line.

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

`LOGIC = (A AND B AND NOT OP0) OR ((A OR B) AND OP0)`. It computes both AND and OR, and lets OP0 pick one, the same shape as the multiplexer in the workbench project.

</details>

## Part 4: Choose with OP1

OUT should be SUM when OP1 is 0 and LOGIC when OP1 is 1. One more multiplexer. When every row matches, the Lab says "Solved".

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

`OUT = (SUM AND NOT OP1) OR (LOGIC AND OP1)`. Then in the chain panel, OP1 = 1, OP0 = 0 on 12 and 10 gives 8 (1100 AND 1010), and OP0 = 1 gives 14 (1100 OR 1010).

</details>

<KeyIdea>An ALU doesn't decide what to compute. It computes everything, and multiplexers pass on the result the operation code selects.</KeyIdea>

## Part 5: Check it on bytes

With your slice solved, use the chain panel to fill in this table before reading the answers:

<TruthTable caption='Your 8-bit ALU' rowsJson='[["A", "B", "operation", "result", "carry out"], ["100", "27", "ADD", "?", "?"], ["100", "27", "SUB", "?", "?"], ["5", "9", "SUB", "?", "?"], ["240", "15", "OR", "?", "?"]]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- 100 + 27 is 127, carry 0.
- 100 - 27 is 73, carry 1 (in subtraction a carry of 1 means no borrow was needed).
- 5 - 9 is 252, the byte for -4, carry 0 (a borrow was needed).
- 240 OR 15 is 255.

Your carry out on subtraction matches the tiny CPU's carry flag after SUB: 1 when no borrow was needed.

</details>
