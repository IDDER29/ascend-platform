You've seen that each instruction is a few steps on the bus, and that every step is one value moving from the part whose "out" signal is on to every part whose "in" signal is on. This lesson follows the arithmetic instructions, ADD and SUB, one signal at a time, and asks a question that's easy to skip: what stays the same?

By the end you'll be able to run ADD and SUB by their control signals, say what each step changes, and predict both flags afterwards.

## The control signals

Each part of the tiny CPU has short names for its signals. These are the ones arithmetic needs:

<TruthTable caption='Control signals for arithmetic' rowsJson='[["signal", "means"], ["CO", "program counter out, onto the bus"], ["MI", "memory address register in, from the bus"], ["RO", "RAM out: the box the memory address register points at"], ["II", "instruction register in"], ["CE", "program counter + 1"], ["IO", "instruction register out: its low 4 bits, the operand"], ["BI", "register B in"], ["EO", "ALU out: A + B, or A - B with SU"], ["AI", "register A in"], ["SU", "make the ALU subtract"], ["FI", "flags in: set zero and carry from the ALU result"]]' />

Every instruction starts with the same fetch: **CO MI**, then **RO II CE**. After it, the instruction register holds the instruction and the program counter points at the next one.

## ADD, step by step

ADD 15 adds the byte in box 15 to A. After the fetch it takes three more steps:

<TruthTable caption='ADD 15 after the fetch' rowsJson='[["step", "signals", "what moves", "what changes"], ["2", "IO MI", "15, from the instruction", "the memory address register becomes 15"], ["3", "RO BI", "the byte in box 15", "B becomes that byte"], ["4", "EO AI FI", "A + B, from the ALU", "A becomes the sum; zero and carry are set"]]' />

Just as important is what doesn't change. Box 15 is only read, so it keeps its value. The program counter isn't touched after the fetch, so the next instruction is still the one after ADD. And A doesn't change until step 4: during steps 2 and 3 the ALU is already showing A + B on its output, but nothing loads it until AI is on.

<KeyIdea>A register only changes on a step where its "in" signal is on. Everything else holds its value, even while new values flow past it on the bus.</KeyIdea>

## The flags come from the result

FI copies two facts about the ALU's result into the flags:

- <b>zero</b> is 1 when the result is exactly 0;
- <b>carry</b> is 1 when the true sum needed a ninth bit.

So ADD with 200 and 100 gives A = 44 with carry 1, because 300 doesn't fit in 8 bits. ADD with 28 and 14 gives 42 with both flags 0. The flags only change on a step with FI, which is why a later LDA leaves them as they were: a conditional jump can still look at the result of the last ADD or SUB.

## SUB is ADD with one more signal

SUB 15 runs exactly the same steps, with SU switched on in the last one:

<TruthTable caption='SUB 15 after the fetch' rowsJson='[["step", "signals"], ["2", "IO MI"], ["3", "RO BI"], ["4", "EO AI SU FI"]]' />

SU makes the ALU invert B and add 1 as it adds: A + NOT B + 1, which is A - B in two's complement. No new circuit, no new steps.

The carry flag means something new after a subtraction. Carry 1 means no borrow was needed, and carry 0 means the result went below zero and wrapped:

<TruthTable caption='Three subtractions' rowsJson='[["A", "box 15", "A after SUB 15", "zero", "carry"], ["9", "5", "4", "0", "1, no borrow"], ["7", "7", "0", "1", "1, no borrow"], ["3", "5", "254 (the byte for -2)", "0", "0, a borrow"]]' />

<KeyIdea>ADD and SUB are the same three steps. SU makes the ALU subtract, and FI saves zero and carry for the next jump to read.</KeyIdea>
