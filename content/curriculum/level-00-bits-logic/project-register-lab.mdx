In this project you build the two most common clocked circuits in a computer: a register with a load input, and a counter. Each Clock Lab gives you the inputs for every tick and checks your registers after each one.

Before each Tick, predict what every register will be. That's the point of the project: the lab only confirms what you worked out.

## Part 1: A register that waits for LOAD

A one-bit register Q should take the value of D on a tick when LOAD is 1, and keep its value on a tick when LOAD is 0. The starting line, `Q := D`, copies D on every tick. Fix it.

<ClockLab caption='A one-bit register with LOAD' inputsJson='["LOAD","D"]' registersJson='["Q"]' startJson='["Q := D"]' targetJson='["Q := (D AND LOAD) OR (Q AND NOT LOAD)"]' ticksJson='[{"LOAD":1,"D":1},{"LOAD":0,"D":0},{"LOAD":0,"D":1},{"LOAD":1,"D":0},{"LOAD":0,"D":1}]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

`Q := (D AND LOAD) OR (Q AND NOT LOAD)`. It's a multiplexer choosing between D and Q, with LOAD as the select. Q goes 1, 1, 1, 0, 0: it only changes on ticks 1 and 4, where LOAD is 1. With `Q := D`, ticks 2 and 5 are wrong, because the register follows D even when it shouldn't.

</details>

## Part 2: Four bits, loaded together

A real register holds several bits that load on the same tick. Write four lines, one per bit, each with the same LOAD.

<ClockLab caption='A 4-bit register' inputsJson='["LOAD","D3","D2","D1","D0"]' registersJson='["Q3","Q2","Q1","Q0"]' startJson='["Q3 := (D3 AND LOAD) OR (Q3 AND NOT LOAD)"]' targetJson='["Q3 := (D3 AND LOAD) OR (Q3 AND NOT LOAD)","Q2 := (D2 AND LOAD) OR (Q2 AND NOT LOAD)","Q1 := (D1 AND LOAD) OR (Q1 AND NOT LOAD)","Q0 := (D0 AND LOAD) OR (Q0 AND NOT LOAD)"]' ticksJson='[{"LOAD":1,"D3":1,"D2":0,"D1":1,"D0":0},{"LOAD":0,"D3":0,"D2":1,"D1":1,"D0":1},{"LOAD":1,"D3":0,"D2":1,"D1":1,"D0":0},{"LOAD":0,"D3":1,"D2":1,"D1":1,"D0":1}]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

The same line four times, one per bit. The register holds 1010, keeps it through tick 2, loads 0110 on tick 3 and keeps it through tick 4. Changing D without LOAD does nothing; that's what lets a register hold a value while other parts of the machine are busy.

</details>

## Part 3: A counter with enable and reset

A 3-bit counter should add 1 on each tick when EN is 1, wrap from 7 back to 0, and go to 0 on any tick when RESET is 1. The starting lines count, but Q2 never changes and RESET is ignored.

<ClockLab caption='A 3-bit counter' inputsJson='["EN","RESET"]' registersJson='["Q2","Q1","Q0"]' startJson='["Q0 := Q0 XOR EN","Q1 := Q1 XOR (Q0 AND EN)","Q2 := Q2"]' targetJson='["Q0 := NOT RESET AND (Q0 XOR EN)","Q1 := NOT RESET AND (Q1 XOR (Q0 AND EN))","Q2 := NOT RESET AND (Q2 XOR (Q1 AND Q0 AND EN))"]' ticksJson='[{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":0,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":0},{"EN":1,"RESET":1},{"EN":1,"RESET":0}]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

- `Q0 := NOT RESET AND (Q0 XOR EN)`
- `Q1 := NOT RESET AND (Q1 XOR (Q0 AND EN))`
- `Q2 := NOT RESET AND (Q2 XOR (Q1 AND Q0 AND EN))`

A bit flips when every bit below it is 1, which is how adding 1 carries in binary. The count goes 1, 2, 3, then holds at 3 (EN is 0 on tick 4), then 4, 5, 6, 7, wraps to 0, stays 0 on the reset tick, and starts again at 1.

</details>

## Part 4: Find the first wrong tick

This counter has one wrong line. Press All ticks, and find the **first** tick that doesn't match before you look at the code.

<ClockLab caption='A broken counter' inputsJson='["EN"]' registersJson='["Q2","Q1","Q0"]' startJson='["Q0 := Q0 XOR EN","Q1 := Q1 XOR EN","Q2 := Q2 XOR (Q1 AND Q0 AND EN)"]' targetJson='["Q0 := Q0 XOR EN","Q1 := Q1 XOR (Q0 AND EN)","Q2 := Q2 XOR (Q1 AND Q0 AND EN)"]' ticksJson='[{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1},{"EN":1}]' />

<details>
<summary>Check your answer</summary>

Tick 1: the counter shows 3 (011) instead of 1 (001), because Q1 flips on every tick. The fix is `Q1 := Q1 XOR (Q0 AND EN)`. After tick 1 every row is wrong, but they're all consequences of that one line: a wrong state at one tick makes every later tick start from the wrong place. That's why you look for the first difference, not the last.

</details>

<KeyIdea>A register changes only on the tick, and only as its := line says. To debug one, find the first tick that differs.</KeyIdea>

## Done when

All four labs say "Solved", you predicted each tick before pressing it, and you can say why the broken counter's first wrong tick was tick 1. Then do the assignment: a counter that counts down.
